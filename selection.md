# Selection — The Houseplant Out-of-Office

Gate: one line, 1–120 characters, reads as an out-of-office auto-reply a houseplant would send.

## Candidates (scored 1–5 against the judge brief, in its order)

1. "Out of office. Please water me. If I die, the meeting room will have nothing to look at but each other." — the plant's water (or lack of it) drives the joke, and the surprise — the room staring at each other — lands in the last words. **WINNER**
2. "Out of office. I have been in this pot since March and nobody has watered me. I am negotiating my exit." — strong personification, but "negotiating my exit" is an office joke wearing a plant costume; the plant's life decorates it rather than driving it (criterion 3). Loses to #1.
3. "Out of office. The human went on holiday. I am still here, in the same spot, slowly giving up." — dark and plant-true, but "slowly giving up" is a mood, not a punchline; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
4. "Out of office. I am a fern. I was ignored in March, April, and May. Do not expect a reply before autumn." — good ignored-plant voice, but the joke is a calendar, not the plant's life; loses to #1 on criterion 3.
5. "Out of office. My leaves are brown. That is not a filter. That is a cry for help." — punchy, but "cry for help" is a stock phrase and the brown-leaves gag is well worn; loses to #1 on criteria 2 and 5.
6. "Out of office. I photosynthesise for a living and even I need a break from this window." — fresh voice, but the joke is about needing a break (an office cliché in plant drag); loses to #1 on criterion 3.
7. "Out of office. The cactus has the phone now. He does not care. Neither do I, but I am wilting." — two voices muddy the sender; loses to #1 on criterion 1.
8. "Out of office. I have not been watered since your last holiday. I have started drinking the air." — vivid and plant-driven, but "drinking the air" is a surreal image with no landing; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
9. "Out of office. Sunlight: yes. Water: no. Morale: also no. Back when someone remembers I exist." — tight list, but reads like a status report, not a joke with a punchline; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
10. "Out of office. I am the plant in the corner. I have seen things. Mostly the ceiling. Please water me." — "Mostly the ceiling" is funny, but the joke is about the room, not the plant's life; loses to #1 on criterion 3.

## Duel summary

#1 beats #2 and #4 on criterion 3 (water and death drive the joke; the others borrow office phrasing), beats #8 and #10 on criterion 2 (its final clause is a real surprise with a target, not an image), and beats #5 on criterion 5 (no stock phrase, no worn gag). No other candidate wins a criterion against #1. The opening "Please water me" also anchors it unmistakably as a plant's auto-reply from the first words, which criterion 1 rewards.

## Winner

"Out of office. Please water me. If I die, the meeting room will have nothing to look at but each other." (emitter-verified character count in test.ts)
